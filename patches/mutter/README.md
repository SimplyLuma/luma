
`0014-constraints-Keep-every-window-inside-one-monitor-work-area.patch` adds a
highest-priority constraint: every normal window, dialog, utility and splash
screen is fully inside the work area of the monitor it overlaps most (else the
nearest), shrunk to fit but not below its minimum size, from its first frame.
It covers app-requested positions and sizes (X11 hints and ConfigureRequests,
Electron restoring saved bounds), placement, monitor changes, un-minimize and
late Wayland configure acknowledgements, which are now constrained outside a
drag. The person moving or resizing a window is exempt, and a window the person
left partly outside stays there until something else moves it or the monitors
change. Tested by the app-requested position sweep in
`tests/tiling-shell/window-memory/resume-trace.js`.

`0015-compositor-Cache-the-blurred-backdrop-and-repaint-only-what-changed.patch`
keeps, per blurred surface and view, the backdrop at the blur's downscaled
resolution and updates only the framebuffer blocks repainted beneath the
surface, so a redraw touching a blurred window grows by the damage plus the
blur's sampling reach instead of the window's whole sampled area. Before this,
a drag near stacked Frost or Glass windows repainted most of both monitors and
re-blurred every blurred window each frame. The cache is rebuilt from a full
repaint on move, resize, scale or radius changes and after a culled frame;
offscreen paints keep the uncached path. `LUMA_MUTTER_BLUR_CACHE=0` disables it
for A/B runs; `MUTTER_DEBUG=render` logs each blurred paint. Measured by
`tests/gnome-shell/blur-motion/`.

`0016-display-Let-the-shell-reserve-work-area-on-any-monitor-edge.patch`: `meta_display_set_monitor_edge_reservation(display, monitor, side, depth)` and `meta_display_clear_monitor_edge_reservations()`. A reservation keeps one monitor's work area `depth` clear of one edge, shared with another monitor or not; Mutter drops struts on shared edges. The Luma Shell (0171) reserves Dash bands on shared edges with it (the sides of a middle display); outer edges keep struts. The work-area inset of 0011 still applies only to edges nothing else moved in. Exercised by the Shell oracle `tests/gnome-shell/shelf-arrange/edges3.js` and `tiling3.js` on three monitors (maximize and Tiling Shell tiles).

`0019-input-Adjustable-scroll-speed-for-mice-and-touchpads.patch`: per-device scroll speed, applied where libinput hands scroll events to Mutter so every Wayland and Xwayland client and the Shell follow it. Settings `org.projectluma.peripherals.mouse` and `.touchpad` `scroll-speed` (0.25–4.0, default 1.0; schema shipped in mutter-common) split devices like natural-scroll: touchpads use the touchpad value, every other pointer the mouse one. Finger and continuous deltas are multiplied (the end-of-gesture zero stays zero, so client kinetic scrolling stays proportional); wheel value120 is multiplied with the sub-unit remainder kept per device and dropped on reversal, and delivered at most one detent per event. At 1.0 the upstream calls run unchanged. The arithmetic is tested by `src/tests/scroll-scale-test.c` in the package's `%check`, which requires all six tests to pass. Same approach as KWin's per-device scroll factor.

`0020-x11-Keep-the-geometry-the-shell-chose-before-map.patch`: a shell that places a new window in its initial `configure` signal gives a frame rectangle, and `meta_window_apply_config()` applies it as one, but `meta_window_x11_configure()` also stored it in the size hints, which hold a client rectangle. When a decorated window's frame arrived, `meta_window_x11_initialize_state()` read those hints back as a client rectangle and grew them by the decorations, so a window placed in a tile briefly became one title bar taller, was pushed back inside the work area, and had to be moved again. The size hints now get the matching client rectangle, and a decorated window keeps the chosen frame rectangle until its frame is there. Windows the shell does not place are configured as before. Measured by `tests/tiling-shell/open-in-place/` ("sized once, at its tile").

`0023-wayland-text-input-Keep-every-commit-and-deletion-until-done.patch`: text-input-v3 applies one deletion and one commit string per done, and a second `commit_string` before the done replaces the first (mutter#5087, the problem mutter!5360 addresses). Commits are appended and sent with the done; a deletion after a pending commit or deletion sends that done first. The surrounding text kept for character-to-byte conversion is advanced by what each done sends, so a deletion that follows before the client reports again is measured against the client's text. Pending text goes to the old focus before a focus change. Exercised by the Shell's `keycap-keypath-native.js` ("several commits in one turn").

`0024-clutter-frame-clock-Dispatch-unpresented-frames-at-the-refresh-rate.patch`: after a dispatch that painted nothing, the presentation-based schedule saw an idle period and dispatched again at once, so anything scheduling updates without damage (a timeline over content that does not paint) spun at main-loop speed (measured on the Fold: 5,000–56,000 dispatches in ten seconds with a few hundred paints, starving idle sources). Such dispatches now come no sooner than one refresh interval after the previous one. The `/frame-clock/unpresented-rate` conformance test (54,545 dispatches in 250 ms without the fix, 15 with it) runs in `%check`.

`0025-display-Let-the-shell-keep-an-on-screen-keyboard-clear-of-windows.patch`: `meta_display_set_input_panel_inset(display, monitor, height)` and `meta_display_get_input_panel_inset()`. The work area of that monitor ends above the keyboard and full-screen windows on it end there too while staying full screen (Android's adjustResize); windows are re-constrained at once and a monitor keeps at least half its height. Used by the Shell keyboard in Mobile and Tablet (0297).

`0026-backends-native-Clamp-the-frame-threads-minimum-utilization.patch`: `org.gnome.mutter frame-utilization-min` (0-1024, default 0) sets uclamp_min on the main thread and the KMS thread with `sched_setattr()`, which the kernel allows a task on its own threads without privileges; SCHED_RESET_ON_FORK keeps threads and processes started later from inheriting it, real-time threads are left alone, and the clamp acts only while the thread is runnable. With schedutil the first frame after idle no longer waits for the frequency ramp (Fold: press frames median 9.9 ms to 2.6-2.9 ms, none over budget). rtkit does not cover this: the KMS thread's default is a nice level, and the main thread cannot be real-time. Luma sets 512 in `config/desktop/dconf/db/luma.d/00-luma-desktop` (shared by desktop and handheld) and, for the greeter's own dconf profile, in `config/desktop/dconf/db/gdm.d/00-prairie-login`; kernels need `CONFIG_UCLAMP_TASK=y`. Tested by `tests/gnome-shell/presentation/frame-utilization-native.js`.

`0027-cogl-Scissor-journal-draws-to-what-they-cover.patch`: each journal draw call narrows its clip stack's scissor to the window-space bounding box of the quads it draws (the clip and stencil are unchanged, so nothing drawn changes). Tiling drivers (v3d, panfrost, freedreno) size a render pass by the scissor; a pass that resumes after something read the framebuffer mid-frame, such as the Shell's backdrop blur, loaded and stored every tile of the framebuffer. Raspberry Pi 5 at 3840x2160: the passes after the blurred dock islands went from 15-19 ms to 0.1-0.7 ms each. Mutter .18.

`0028-clutter-actor-Do-not-redraw-the-whole-stage-for-a-first-paint.patch`: an actor whose visible paint volume was never computed has not been painted, so its first layout adds only its new volume to the redraw clip. Before, it counted as a moved actor with an unknown old volume and queued a full stage redraw, and with buffer-age damage history the next frames repainted the whole output too. Every new actor did this: the Shell's dock swaps each icon's texture actor on hover, so a dock hover redrew the whole 3840x2160 output on a Raspberry Pi 5 nearly every frame. Mutter .19.

`0029-stage-impl-Clip-compact-damage-with-the-scissor-alone.patch`: when a clipped redraw's rectangles cover at least half of their bounding box, the stage paints the box with a scissor clip instead of a stencil clip of the rectangles. Cogl clears and redraws a stencil clip every time painting returns to the onscreen framebuffer (after each offscreen effect or blur), and on Mesa without a scissored quad clear the stencil clear loaded and stored the whole framebuffer. Tiling GPUs load and store every tile of the box either way. Mutter .20.

`0030-backends-native-Probe-input-again-when-a-session-starts-inactive.patch`: a compositor started on a session that is not active yet never paused (the launcher reads Active before the backend connects to its changes), so the input devices its seat opened, which logind revokes for an inactive session, were never probed again, and the first activation only logged "Spurious call to meta_seat_native_reclaim_devices()": no keyboard or pointer until the session restarted. Luma's GDM (0001-luma-setup-handover-vt) starts the first session after first-boot setup on an inactive VT, so this hit every first session. When the session is inactive once the seat has started, its devices are released at once and the activation reclaims them. Sessions that start active are unchanged. Mutter .21.

`0031-wayland-Let-the-compositor-drag-data-to-clients.patch`: `MetaDataDrag` (`meta/meta-data-drag.h`), a drag source owned by the compositor. Only a client's `wl_data_source` could start a Wayland drag, so nothing the Shell draws could be dropped on a client. A `MetaDataDrag` offers MIME types, with content set up front or later (`meta_data_drag_offer()`, then `meta_data_drag_set_content()`; requests that arrive first wait), and `meta_data_drag_begin()` starts the regular drag grab from a pointer button's press or motion or a touch point. Wayland clients get a `wl_data_offer`; X11 clients get the drag through the XDND bridge, which reads the DND selection as for any Wayland drag. Over anything that is not a client surface, `motion` returns the action a compositor drop would perform (the cursor shows it) and `drop` lets the compositor take the drop; `ended` says how the grab ended (cancelled, dropped on a client, dropped on the compositor) and `finished` when the content stops being offered (after the client's `finish`, a cancel or 60 s). `meta_data_drag_set_actions(actions, preferred)`: with no modifier a target gets `preferred`; Shift and Control still choose move and copy. Escape and a cancelled touch point cancel. The drag grab accepts a drag with no origin surface. Upstream's Clutter drag-and-drop framework (mutter!5232, draft) would make Wayland surfaces Clutter drop targets and supersede this; until it lands this is the smallest API that lets the Shell hand data to clients. Used by the Shell's `lumaFileDrag.js` (0348). Mutter .22.

`0032-wayland-Send-a-configure-without-resizing-when-a-resize-ends.patch`: backport of upstream's fix in Mutter 50.6. The configure Mutter sends when an interactive resize ends was still marked `resizing`, and nothing followed it: `meta_window_wayland_grab_op_ended()` builds it while the drag is still the compositor's current drag (cleared only after the drag's `ended` signal). `MetaWindowDrag` now records that it is ending (`meta_window_drag_is_ending()`), and `fill_states()` leaves `resizing` out of that configure. Identical to 50.6's code, so it drops out when Luma moves to 50.6. Exercised by `tests/mutter/window-shape/run.sh` (every drag ends with a configure without `resizing`). Mutter .23.

`0033-wayland-Let-a-client-keep-its-window-at-an-aspect-ratio.patch`: `luma_window_shape_v1` (`src/luma-platform/protocols/luma-window-shape-v1.xml`, installed into `src/wayland/protocol/` in `%prep` like the background-effect XML), the Wayland counterpart of the ICCCM aspect hints Mutter already honours for X11 clients. `get_window_shape(wl_surface)` gives a surface a shape object; `set_aspect_ratio(width, height, base_width, base_height)` and `unset_aspect_ratio` are double-buffered and reach the window's size hints on the toplevel's next commit. The ratio is kept in `size_hints.min_aspect`/`max_aspect`, so `constrain_aspect_ratio` applies it to every size Mutter picks while the window floats: each step of an interactive resize from any edge or corner (the client never sees an off-ratio configure), sizes the app asks for, and the work-area fit of 0014, which now shrinks such a window in proportion. Unlike ICCCM hints it is measured on the frame rectangle (the window geometry) less the base, the client's fixed chrome such as a header bar, so a game view below a header bar keeps its shape. Maximize: the window becomes the largest size with that ratio inside the area it would otherwise fill (work area less the 0017 insets), centred: full height for a window narrower than the screen. Tiles: the ratio is not applied to side-by-side tiles or windows a tiling manager marked tiled (0008); the window fills its tile and the client letterboxes, because a tile's size belongs to the layout and a shorter window would leave a gap its neighbours do not fill. Fullscreen: not applied. A wl_surface is taken rather than an xdg_toplevel because GTK keeps its xdg_toplevel private; the state applies only while the surface is an xdg_toplevel. A separate global, rather than a new gtk_surface1 request, so Luma never collides with upstream's gtk-shell versions. A window a tiling manager marked tiled now also ignores X11 aspect hints, as Mutter's own side-by-side tiles already did. Client side: `luma_window_set_aspect_ratio()` in LumaUI. Tested by `tests/mutter/window-shape/run.sh` (headless GNOME Shell, real pointer drags). Mutter .23.
